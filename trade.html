Who gets paid when buyer and supplier disagree

A Trade Assurance-style order settled on Takar Settle. The buyer's payment sits in a contract. The supplier is paid only when independent specialists, bought through Telegraph, agree that the conditions are met. When they disagree, nothing moves until someone looks. Once either party disputes, the decision needs an outside seat held by another team (Praman) to back it.

The order is illustrative: the supplier is not a real company, and the amount is test USDC scaled 1:12,400. The ship, its AIS positions, the miners, every paid answer, the escrow and the bonds are real, on Ethereum Sepolia testnet.

The conditions, and who checks them

Ship arrived

Two vessel miners on different operators (Quay, a terrestrial AIS network; VesselWire, Fintraffic Digitraffic) both put the named ship berthed at the destination port: within 1.5 km, under 1 knot, a reading under 60 minutes old. Arrival alone never pays. It proves the ship is in port, not that the goods were delivered or accepted. From 27 Nov the port's own record must agree as well (Portnet arrival and departure times via Digitraffic, declared by the ship's agent and the port): two AIS relays are still one broadcast from the ship, so spoofed AIS alone cannot pay. With a delivery date in the terms, a late arrival goes to the panel.

Goods accepted

The buyer's goods-received note is the acceptance record. Two reconcile miners on different operators compare the supplier's invoice with the purchase order and that note. In the quantity case, Praman's own three-way reconciliation must land on the same shortfall.

Supplier clear

Two sanctions-screening miners on different operators agree, and Praman's own OFAC SDN screen lands the same way.

Pay when everything passes. Hold while both vessel miners and the port's own record agree the ship is not in port. Review when any pair disagrees or an answer is unusable: nothing is proposed. Partial (quantity case) pays for the accepted units only, when both miners and Praman agree on the shortfall; the rest goes back to the buyer. Refund when nothing can be paid. A proposal opens a 10-minute dispute window only after two of the three resolvers back the same decision on the same evidence. A party disputes by posting a 0.5 USDC bond, lost to the other side if the payout it disputed stands. Once a party has disputed, a decision needs Praman's seat: on TakarSettle5 (case 2) the contract itself requires all three resolvers after any dispute; on the older TakarSettle4 (case 1) that is Takar's policy (its second key steps aside), and the contract requires all three only after both parties dispute.

In trade-finance terms

Like a letter of credit, checked by machines

Under a documentary credit (ICC UCP 600, art. 14) a bank pays on documents that comply on their face, and a discrepancy stops payment until it is resolved or waived (art. 16). Takar works the same way with independent checkers in place of one bank: the parties' signed documents, the port's record and the miners' answers are the documents, a disagreement between them is a discrepancy (REVIEW), and the panel with an outside seat is who may waive it. Unlike a bank's examination, every check here has a public receipt and can be re-decided by anyone from the case file.

Where delivery happens (Incoterms 2020)

These orders are DAP Helsinki West Harbour: the seller delivers when the goods arrive at the named place, ready for unloading. Delivery under the Incoterm is not the same as the buyer accepting the goods, and payment here is tied to acceptance (the buyer's signed goods-received note), which is why arrival alone never pays. Under FOB or FCA the arrival check would move to the port of loading; the rest of the rules stay the same.

What is not covered yet

Quality inspection (a third-party inspection certificate would be one more signed document), electronic bills of lading (DCSA eBL) as the title document, and claims after acceptance. Takar's panel decides payment, not liability: it does not replace a court or an arbitration clause.
