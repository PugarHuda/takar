Takar

Each machine below had a budget for intelligence. Before moving, it bought the checks its task needed through Telegraph's ranked market, compared them with the nearest airport weather station, and decided whether to go.

Simulated fleet, real purchases. The units are illustrative. Every purchase settled on Ethereum Sepolia testnet with test USDC, and every station reading is a published METAR report.

What the receipts showed

  1. The top-ranked storm miner ignores the place you ask about. Storm checks for Makassar, Palangka Raya and Bandung were routed to miner 37007, which answered every one with Tropical Cyclone AKVAN-26 in India. Once, that answer was shaped into a 296 km/h gust for Bandung. Reported with receipts in bounties#211.
  2. A storm answer can be paid for and empty. The next-ranked miner answered Palangka Raya with every field blank, so the drone there held.
  3. A station can disagree with a recorded-weather answer. A miner gave Makassar a 31.9 °C high for 15 November; the Hasanuddin airport station recorded 34 °C across 49 reports.
  4. When two independent sources agree, the machine goes. The Makassar drone flew after the storm miner forecast a 22.3 km/h gust and the airport station measured 17 km/h.

Conditional payments (Missions 14 and 06)

Test USDC locked in TakarSettle v2 against a condition. Takar buys the answer through Telegraph and proposes a settlement; the payer and the payee each get one dispute during a 10-minute window, and then anyone can finalize it. The contract refuses an event proposal backed by fewer than two Telegraph signals, so two different miners must agree. A drought cover also needs NASA POWER, a record independent of Telegraph's weather miners, to land on the same side of the trigger, or it holds.

A second opinion for any Telegraph intent

Every team on Telegraph ends up writing its own cross-check, because the ranked route can send a question to the wrong specialist or come back confidently wrong. Takar does it once, for any intent: it asks two different miners, chosen by an open on-chain record of who was right, and compares their answers field by field. agree, disagree or insufficient, never an average. Free to call (Takar pays the asks in test USDC). Use it from any agent: OpenAPI spec for HTTP tools such as Minds, or npx -y takar-mcp (tool second_opinion).

Who was right: the open record

Every check Takar bought is an EAS attestation on Ethereum Sepolia: the miner, the Telegraph signal as evidence, and whether the answer held up. Anyone can add evidence to the same schema. Paid asks are ordered only by attesters Takar trusts. An honest "unresolved" is coverage, not error.
