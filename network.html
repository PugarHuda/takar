Telegraph network check

Three things a buyer of Telegraph answers should know and cannot read from a leaderboard: who really runs the miners, how often two independent miners disagree, and whether a paid answer can be traced from the node to an on-chain anchor. Read live from the node's public registry and Takar's records on Ethereum Sepolia. Takar board · API: /api/network?view=operators|disagreement|signal

Who runs the miners

An operator here is a fee address. Miners that pay the same address count as one operator: that may be one team or a platform account, and the registry does not say which. A second opinion is only independent across operators.

Different operators, same upstream

The host each miner's registry entry calls. When miners on different payout addresses call the same API, two of them agreeing is one source relayed twice. Takar's second opinion reports this as "shared upstream".

Independent choice per intent

How many operators actually serve each intent. Effective operators is the inverse Herfindahl index: the number of equal-sized operators the intent's miners amount to.

How often independent miners disagree

Every second opinion Takar gives compares two miners from different operators on the same question, and is recorded on EAS. Pairs where one answer was unusable are left out. The interval is a 95% Wilson interval.

Miner status, as a buyer sees it

Every miner Takar paid in the last 7 days, from its own ledger. Answered means a paid result came back; complete means every field the intent needs came back ok. The floor is the low end of the 95% interval, so three lucky asks do not outrank thirty steady ones. Measured by a paying client, not reported by the operator.

Trace a signal

Paste any Telegraph signal hash. It is looked up on the node, in Takar's paid ledger, and in the Merkle roots of that ledger Takar anchors on EAS; the root is recomputed from the published file, so an edited or deleted receipt would not match.
